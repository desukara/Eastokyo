About Eastokyo

Not Here
to Behave

Eastokyo is an independent magazine about the real Tokyo, not the version people think they know because of Pokémon or Hello Kitty. We’ll be the first to admit we love sushi, matcha, ninjas, and geishas as much as the next person. But everyone already knows about that stuff, and it’s not really what Tokyo’s about. That’s just the highlight reel. We’re here to show you the rest of it.

This isn’t your typical magazine, and we’re not here to sell you the fantasy that Tokyo’s got it all figured out, because friends, nobody does.

Eastokyo is written and photographed by James Simmons, plus the occasional guest author. We’re not the magazine hyping up Tokyo with stories about giant sumo wrestlers, jaw-dropping Mount Fuji sunsets, or traditional tea ceremonies. Tokyo is a megacity with real problems, just like any other major city on Earth. The only difference is the social contract here says keep it to yourself, which makes it a lot easier to sell Tokyo as some kind of complaint-free utopia.

Everything's supposed to run at maximum efficiency, 24 hours a day, thanks to a strict social order and a pile of unspoken rules. That version of reality is based on a true story, but it’s mostly exaggerated for effect. Tokyo is full of regular people, same as anywhere else in the world. The big difference is the social pressure to keep everything looking clean on the surface. We’re here to peek behind that surface and have a laugh at what's actually going on.

The August Issue: Clean on the Surface

The first full issue looks at the gap between Tokyo’s carefully managed public image and what’s actually going on backstage. Seven stories dig into social appearances, foreign workers, loneliness, belonging, workplace culture, conformity, and the myth of the submissive Japanese woman.

The stories are written and photographed by James Simmons in Tokyo, Japan, with the guts and the good humour to tell you what’s really going on instead of some fairy-tale version of the city.
