Contribute to Eastokyo

BRING THE PROFESSION INTO CLEARER VIEW.

Eastokyo welcomes reported ideas, informed essays, professional expertise, sources, photography and recurring editorial projects.

You do not need an established byline. You do need a specific idea, honesty about what you know, respect for evidence and a willingness to revise.

Teachers and school leaders · researchers and writers · sources and photographers

Start with what you know, notice or can help uncover.

A useful contribution may begin with one classroom moment, a document, an unanswered question, years of experience or access to a story that has not been reported properly.

An editor can help test the idea, identify what evidence is needed and decide whether it should become an essay, reported feature, interview, visual story, case study or recurring format.

Accuracy, fairness, disclosure, consent and clear sourcing apply to every route into the magazine.

What to send

A strong first email can still be brief.

01

Introduce yourself and your connection to the subject.

Name your current or past role, relevant experience and any conflicts or relationships Eastokyo should know about.

02

Describe the idea in one paragraph.

Explain the question, event, person or problem—and why it matters to readers now.

03

Say what you can bring.

That may be direct experience, interviews, documents, research, photographs, access or a clear reporting plan.

04

Flag practical needs early.

State whether confidentiality, anonymity, a deadline, simultaneous submission or prior publication is relevant.

What happens next

Clear expectations before substantial work begins.

Editorial review

An editor may ask questions, request a fuller pitch, suggest a different format, commission a draft or decline the idea. Submission does not guarantee publication.

Response time

Eastokyo aims to acknowledge pitches within ten business days. Time-sensitive approaches should say so in the subject line.

Payment

Compensation varies by assignment and is confirmed in writing before commissioned work begins. Do not undertake substantial unpaid work on assumption.

Rights and prior publication

Rights, exclusivity, edits and republication terms are agreed before publication. Disclose simultaneous submissions and previously published material at the outset.

Sources and confidentiality

Contacting Eastokyo does not automatically create confidentiality. Flag sensitive circumstances before sharing identifying or restricted information so terms can be discussed first.

Standards

All work is governed by Eastokyo’s editorial policy, including verification, fairness, disclosure, consent and public corrections.
