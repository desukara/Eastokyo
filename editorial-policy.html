Editorial standards

What we owe the reader.

Trust is not a tone of voice. It is the result of how a publication reports, verifies, discloses, protects people and corrects itself when the work falls short.

The promise

We do not ask readers to take our integrity on faith.

Eastokyo reports on a profession shaped by unequal power: teachers may depend on an employer for income, housing or visa stability; schools may face serious reputational consequences; learners and families may be affected by decisions they did not make.

That makes care, evidence and independence operational requirements—not decorative values. We explain relevant relationships, test important claims, distinguish reporting from opinion and avoid publishing private information merely because it is available.

The standard in practice

Seven decisions that define the work.

01 / Evidence

Claims must survive contact with the facts.

Material assertions are checked against documents, direct observation, credible data or people with relevant knowledge. A compelling account is not automatically a complete account. When facts remain uncertain, the story says so.

02 / Fairness

Criticism is tested, not softened.

People and organisations facing meaningful criticism are normally given a reasonable opportunity to respond before publication. Their reply does not determine our conclusion, but it belongs in the reporting when it adds relevant facts or context.

03 / Sources

Anonymity is protection, not theatre.

Unnamed sources are used when the information has genuine public value and identification could expose a person to disproportionate professional or personal harm. Editors must know who the source is, understand their motives and seek independent support for consequential claims.

04 / Privacy

Access does not create entitlement.

We minimise unnecessary identifying details, especially where students, families, junior staff or vulnerable sources are involved. Private messages, employment records and personal documents are not published simply because someone supplied them.

05 / Conflicts

Relationships are disclosed where judgment could be questioned.

Relevant financial, professional, personal and organisational ties are disclosed to editors and, where material to the reader, in the published work. A disclosure does not cure every conflict; some assignments should be reassigned or declined.

06 / Money

Commercial support cannot buy an editorial conclusion.

Sponsored, commissioned and partner-supported work is labelled clearly before the reader enters it. Advertisers and partners may purchase production and distribution, but not undisclosed influence, favourable findings or the suppression of relevant facts.

07 / Corrections

Accuracy continues after publication.

Errors are corrected promptly. Changes that materially affect meaning receive a visible correction note explaining what was wrong and what changed. Quietly repairing a consequential error is not an adequate correction.

Publisher relationship

Englishire publishes Eastokyo. It does not own the conclusion.

Englishire provides temporary English teacher cover and therefore has commercial relationships and operational knowledge within the field Eastokyo reports on. That connection is visible by design and must be handled directly.

Client information is not converted into editorial material without consent, public-interest justification and appropriate reporting. Englishire service interests do not determine story selection, prevent legitimate scrutiny or purchase favourable treatment.

Where the publisher relationship is material to a story, the story will say so.

Corrections and concerns

Challenge the work with specifics.

Editorial complaints are reviewed on their substance, regardless of the status of the person, school or organisation raising them. Disagreement alone does not establish an error, but credible evidence receives a serious review.

  1. 01Send the article URL and identify the exact passage at issue.
  2. 02Explain what is inaccurate, unfair or insufficiently disclosed.
  3. 03Include documents, records or other supporting material where available.
  4. 04Tell us whether there is a safety, privacy or time-sensitive concern.
